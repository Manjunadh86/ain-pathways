How lessons are written and sourced

AIN Pathways is a set of original lessons. This page explains where the material comes from, what was checked, and what was not.

The content contract

Every topic has one lesson with the same parts: a plain definition, why it matters, prerequisites, objectives, an explanation with at least two diagrams, a worked example, edge cases, guidance on when a simpler tool is enough, four or five documented real-world uses, common mistakes, at least one exercise with a solution, and three to six curated resources. A validator refuses to build the site if any lesson is missing a part.

What “real-world example” means here

Each example names a real product, system or documented workflow and cites a page that was fetched and read. Facts stated by the source are listed separately under “What the sources document”. The step-by-step explanation, the reasons and the tradeoffs are our own analysis. Code written to illustrate an example is labelled as a teaching example. Charts say whether their numbers are illustrative, computed from a formula, or taken from a cited source.

What was and was not verified

After a lesson is written, a second reviewer that did not write it fetches the cited pages again and checks each documented claim, then repairs or flags problems. A separate link check records whether each URL was reachable. A reachable link does not prove a claim, and a blocked automated request does not mean a page is broken, so both results are reported as they are. Lessons can still contain mistakes. Follow the sources when something matters.
