Capstone project

Forward Deployed Engagement Simulation

Run a complete customer engagement on paper and in code, from the first discovery call to a handoff the customer can operate.

IntermediateAbout 36 hours (estimate)

The brief

You are the forward deployed engineer on a six-week engagement with a mid-sized logistics company (a hypothetical customer). Their operations team spends hours every day answering "where is my shipment?" emails by looking in three systems. The sponsor has asked for "an AI chatbot".

Play both sides honestly. Write the discovery notes you would take, decide what the real problem is, scope a first slice, build a working prototype on realistic sample data, plan the deployment into the customer's environment and hand it over. Ask a friend or colleague to play the customer for the discovery interview and the demo if you can.

The output is a portfolio piece that includes the documents an FDE writes and the software an FDE ships.

What you build

  • Discovery notes, a problem statement and testable requirements.
  • A scoping document, a solution design document and a stakeholder map.
  • A working prototype that answers shipment questions from sample data through a small front end.
  • An evaluation set and results agreed with the "customer".
  • A deployment plan for the customer's environment, a runbook and a product feedback memo.

Milestones

  1. 1

    Discover, define and scope

    Do the discovery before opening an editor. End this milestone with documents a real sponsor could sign off, including what you will not build.

    Deliverables

    • Interview and observation notes, a solution-free problem statement and testable requirements.
    • An engagement plan with phase exit criteria and a stakeholder map.
    • A scoping document with a two-week first slice, exclusions and risk-ordered sequencing.
    • A baseline measurement and the agreed success metric.

    Covers R1, R2, R3, R4

  2. 2

    Design and integrate

    Write the design document, then build the data layer it describes. Keep the document updated as the build teaches you things.

    Deliverables

    • A solution design document with one architecture decision record.
    • A database schema, a loaded sample data set and a working carrier integration.

    Covers R5, R6

  3. 3

    Prototype, demo and evaluate

    Build the thin end-to-end slice, demo it against a stated hypothesis, then measure it on an evaluation set agreed with the customer.

    Deliverables

    • A working prototype with a front end.
    • Demo notes with the hypothesis, the feedback and the changes made.
    • Evaluation results mapped to each requirement, with traces for the failures.

    Covers R7, R8, R9

  4. 4

    Plan deployment, hand over and feed the product

    Plan how this runs inside the customer's environment, hand it to their team, and tell your own product team what you learned.

    Deliverables

    • A deployment plan with a network and identity diagram and a CI/CD outline.
    • A data handling note.
    • A runbook, service level objectives and a training outline.
    • A product feedback memo.

    Covers R10, R11, R12, R13

Acceptance criteria

The project is done when every statement below is true.

  • The problem statement names a measurable problem and contains no solution.
  • Every requirement can be checked by a test or a measurement, and the evaluation report checks each one.
  • The scope document lists at least three things that are explicitly out of the first slice.
  • The success metric has a baseline measured before the build.
  • The prototype answers shipment questions from the sample data and escalates when the data is missing instead of guessing.
  • Sample data loads repeatably with one command, and running the load twice does not duplicate rows.
  • The deployment plan states where every secret lives and who can access the system.
  • The runbook lets someone who did not build the system diagnose a failed carrier API call.
  • The feedback memo separates reusable product capability from customer-specific work.

Stretch goals

  • Present the engagement as a 15-minute readout to a non-technical audience and collect questions.
  • Add infrastructure-as-code for the deployment plan and run it in your own cloud account.
  • Turn the engagement into an interview case study with the decisions you would defend.
